About Dorcas House

A Christian recovery program for women and children, part of Union Rescue Mission

The Dorcas House of Little Rock, Arkansas is a Christian based long-term recovery program for women and children who have experienced domestic violence and substance abuse. Operating as a sector of the Union Rescue Mission since 1963, it is dedicated to helping women break cycles of abuse and rebuild their lives with a support system to guide them.[4]

Their services include personalized job counseling, therapy, classes, child care, and daily devotional time. They give women a chance to feel like themselves again, making it a natural fit for Woven.

Why this exists

Fast Fashion Creates a Clothing Problem

The fashion industry produces far more clothing than people ultimately use. As production has grown, so has textile waste. Textile production has nearly doubled since 2000, while textile waste in the United States has risen dramatically.[1] Much of that clothing never returns to meaningful use.

The problem extends beyond waste. Clothing is also a basic expense that becomes difficult to prioritize when families are already balancing housing, food, transportation, healthcare, and childcare.

In Pulaski County, 15% of residents live below the poverty line, while another 27% fall into the ALICE category: Asset Limited, Income Constrained, Employed.[3] Dorcas House works directly with women navigating many of these challenges.

Our Research

Designed With the Women Who Will Wear It

Nineteen women at Dorcas House shared what they wanted most in a blouse. Their feedback directly shaped our design.

84%
Preferred a slip-on design, a blouse they could pull over their head rather than a button-front style.
79%
Wanted pockets, one of the clearest preferences in the entire survey.
63%
Preferred a flowy, relaxed fit over a structured or boxy silhouette.

Their feedback helped shape the details too. Fifty-eight percent preferred a longer, tunic-style blouse, while 63% preferred short sleeves. Color preferences were nearly evenly divided among neutrals, earth tones, and shades of blue, green, and navy.

The result is a blouse designed to work for different women, different body types, and different preferences, with a focus on inclusive sizing from XS through XXL.

For Real Life

One Blouse, Many Places

The women were also asked where they hoped to wear the blouse. Church was the most common answer, but their responses went beyond that: job interviews, work, birthday parties, community events, casual outings, traveling, and everyday life. Several simply said "anywhere" or "everywhere."

Their answers made it clear: they wanted a blouse that could move with them through everyday life.

We also asked the women how having the right clothing makes them feel. Their answers spoke to something deeper than simply getting dressed.

The Blend Matters

Why Hemp Makes Sense

What clothing is made from matters, both in how it feels and how long it lasts. Research published in Materials found that hemp-linen blend fabrics can provide durability along with strong air permeability and moisture management. Hemp fibers are also breathable and biodegradable.[5]

For Woven, hemp and hemp blends are preferred whenever practical. They allow us to create clothing that lasts longer, feels comfortable, and avoids some of the waste associated with disposable fashion. Hemp helps tackle textile waste from both ends. Its durability means it can be worn longer and even passed down rather than quickly replaced. And if it does reach a landfill, hemp is biodegradable.

Meet Sadie

The Story Behind Woven

Hi! I'm Sadie Janes.

Sustainability became important to me at a young age. I became a vegetarian in fifth grade, and that early decision made me curious about how small personal choices can create larger environmental effects.

In ninth-grade biology, I researched fast fashion for a quarter project and I found myself returning to the subject in later research papers and assignments. At the end of my junior year, I wanted to understand it more deeply. I spent nearly a month digging further into textile waste, secondhand clothing, clothing insecurity, and sustainable materials.

I wanted my final project to move beyond another research paper. Working with Dorcas House gave me an opportunity to listen to women about what they wanted and use that information to create something tangible.

Woven grew from that experience.

— Sadie
From Insight to Action

A Blouse Built From Answers

The survey produced a clear starting point for the final design. The current concept combines a flowy silhouette with a longer tunic-style length, a slip-on construction, and pockets.

The proposed production mix offers sizes from XS to XXL with a versatile palette of white, navy, and khaki. Every choice from fit and function to color reflects what matters most to the women who will wear it.

Made to Last

Thoughtfully Designed for a Longer Life

Durability matters. Research on garment longevity shows durability is about more than fabric strength. Clothing is kept and worn longer when it also fits the wearer's lifestyle, preferences, climate, and daily needs.[2]

Our research with the women of Dorcas House reflected exactly that. With busy lives, balancing work, motherhood, and new beginnings, they wanted clothing versatile enough for church, work, interviews, social events, and just everyday life.

One blouse. More ways to wear it. More reasons to keep it.
